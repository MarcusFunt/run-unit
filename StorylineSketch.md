# RUN//UNIT — Current Storyline and Campaign Structure

## Core premise

RUN//UNIT begins inside the factory that manufactured the player robot, UNIT-07. The opening is not a heroic mission briefing: UNIT-07 is simply moving through the end of its normal production and calibration process when the facility's automation fails.

The story should be understood primarily from the environment while the player moves. Machinery, stored robots, labels, broken infrastructure, distant landmarks, lighting changes, and the condition of the world should communicate more than exposition does.

The current campaign is deliberately compact:

1. **Tutorial — Calibration**: learn what UNIT-07 can do and leave the malfunctioning calibration area.
2. **Level 1 — Factory Escape**: cross the failed transfer system, discover the warehouse full of similar robots, and escape the factory.
3. **Level 2 — Recovery**: cross the exterior service district, enter a reserve facility, and recover the replacement ignition module.
4. **Level 3 — Beacon 9**: carry the module across the failing city to Beacon 9 and install it.

The replacement ignition module is a **single-purpose mission object**. It is recovered in Level 2, carried visibly through Level 3, and used exactly once: when UNIT-07 installs it into Beacon 9 at the end of the game. It is not a reusable power-up, puzzle tool, weapon, or general-purpose battery.

The exact cause of the wider system failure should remain unexplained unless the game later gains a concrete reason to reveal it.

## Tutorial — Calibration

### Purpose

The tutorial establishes that UNIT-07 is a manufactured machine and teaches the movement needed for the rest of the game without feeling like a detached obstacle course.

### Sequence

UNIT-07 activates in a calibration/testing tunnel. Factory systems guide the robot through basic movement, a normal jump, a charged spring jump, and crouching.

The environment should make each action feel like part of an industrial test rather than a floating tutorial challenge. The calibration area is enclosed, mechanical, and controlled, although signs of failure are already present.

The final crouch lesson is a malfunctioning transfer/elevator door that is stuck partially closed. UNIT-07 crouches beneath it; the antenna physically folds against the low overhead clearance. Once UNIT-07 passes through, the door slams shut and the screen goes dark.

That blackout is the transition into Level 1.

### What the player should understand

- UNIT-07 was built here.
- The factory normally tests robots before sending them onward.
- The facility is already malfunctioning.
- The player now knows the complete core movement vocabulary.

## Level 1 — Factory Escape

### Purpose

Level 1 turns the tutorial controls into real traversal and gives the first major narrative reveal: UNIT-07 is not unique, but one manufactured unit among many.

Target first-play time: roughly **5–7 minutes**.

### 1. Elevator arrival / intact transfer line

The elevator opens into the production-transfer system. This should feel like the next place UNIT-07 would normally have gone after calibration.

The first section is comparatively intact. Stationary conveyor/transfer machinery forms long, readable platforms with easy gaps. The player uses the tutorial mechanics without further control prompts.

### 2. Broken sorting and transfer machinery

The route becomes increasingly misaligned and damaged. Missing conveyor sections, equipment housings, catwalks, tilted machinery, and collapsed structures create the platforming route naturally.

Difficulty should come from combining familiar actions, not from introducing new mechanics. Ordinary gaps remain generous; charged jumps are used deliberately rather than constantly.

### 3. Suspended transfer section

The route rises and exposes a larger drop beneath the production machinery. Longer runs and slightly wider gaps increase momentum and make the factory feel larger.

### 4. Mandatory collapse

A transfer section fails beneath UNIT-07 and drops the player into the storage warehouse below.

The fall is mandatory and safe. It is a narrative set-piece, not a reaction-time trap or punishment.

### 5. Warehouse reveal

UNIT-07 lands on the top of a broad storage rack. The game should briefly reduce difficulty here so the player can look around.

Rows of shelving contain robots that resemble UNIT-07, packaging, storage bays, unit labels, and industrial inventory infrastructure. No text needs to explain the implication: UNIT-07 is a product from a large production system.

### 6. Rack-top traversal

The warehouse becomes the main second half of Level 1. The route moves between several elevations across rack tops, damaged shelving, cross-bracing, crates, and gaps between storage aisles.

The playable path can remain mostly linear while repeated background racks and stored robots create the impression of a much larger facility.

### 7. Damaged exterior edge

As UNIT-07 approaches the outside wall, the warehouse becomes more physically compromised. Shelves are bent or collapsed, exterior light begins leaking into the space, and the factory's internal structure gives way to the outside environment.

The level ends when UNIT-07 crosses the breach in the factory wall.

### What the player should understand

- The production system has failed badly enough that UNIT-07 must traverse it manually.
- Many robots like UNIT-07 were manufactured and stored here.
- The malfunction is larger than one calibration room.
- There is an outside world beyond the factory.

## Level 2 — Recovery

### Purpose

Level 2 expands the world beyond the factory, introduces Beacon 9 as a visual landmark, and gives UNIT-07 a concrete mission: recover the replacement ignition module from a reserve facility.

Beacon 9 may be visible from the beginning of the level, but it should **not yet be explained as the final destination**. The player should first experience it as a mysterious, enormous structure on the skyline.

Target first-play time: roughly **7–9 minutes**.

### 1. Factory exterior

UNIT-07 emerges from the Level 1 breach onto exterior service infrastructure. The opening is intentionally easy so the player can absorb the first wide view of the city and Beacon 9.

This is the first major change in scale: large sky shapes, distant buildings, maintenance structures, and empty infrastructure replace the enclosed factory.

### 2. Exterior service district

UNIT-07 traverses rooftops, ventilation housings, balconies, maintenance walkways, signage structures, and broken service infrastructure.

The route initially descends away from the factory, giving the player a sense of entering the wider city rather than simply continuing through another corridor.

### 3. Reserve facility sightline

A nearby reserve engineering/power facility becomes the immediate visual destination. A surviving maintenance display or system identifies a critical replacement assembly stored there.

The messaging should remain terse, for example:

> CRITICAL REPLACEMENT ASSEMBLY  
> RESERVE DEPOT 03

Beacon 9 remains visible as the larger, unexplained landmark beyond the immediate objective.

### 4. Facility approach

The obvious entrance to the reserve facility is unavailable. UNIT-07 reaches the building through exterior maintenance infrastructure: rooftop machinery, service platforms, damaged access routes, or a broken maintenance opening.

This should remain platforming rather than becoming a switch puzzle.

### 5. Upper reserve facility

Inside, the visual language changes from mass production to heavy infrastructure: cooling equipment, electrical cabinets, thick walls, large pipes, maintenance gantries, and protected storage systems.

Whenever practical, the module should be shown before the player can reach it. Seeing the objective through a shaft, window, or central chamber gives the level a persistent short-term goal.

### 6. Cooling / machinery descent

The route descends through a large internal machinery space using staggered platforms and broad landings. The goal is spatial variety rather than precision falling.

### 7. Core-access ascent

After reaching the lower part of the facility, the route turns upward around the secured storage chamber. This reverses the previous descent and provides the main charged-jump sequence of the level.

The player should repeatedly glimpse the module getting closer.

### 8. Vault perimeter

Difficulty reduces again around the storage chamber. UNIT-07 moves around the edge of the vault while the replacement module remains clearly visible.

This section exists primarily to build anticipation.

### 9. Module acquisition

UNIT-07 reaches the storage cradle and removes the replacement ignition module. The module becomes visibly mounted to the robot and remains visible for the rest of the game.

No reusable device mechanic is introduced.

This is the moment when the larger mission becomes explicit. The facility identifies the required target:

> REPLACEMENT IGNITION MODULE ACQUIRED  
> TARGET SYSTEM: BEACON 9

The structure the player has already seen in the distance now becomes the final destination.

### 10. Reserve shutdown / exit

Removing the module changes the reserve facility's state. Emergency lighting, shutters, or other systems may react because the protected reserve component has been removed, but **the module itself has still not been used**.

UNIT-07 leaves by a shorter maintenance return route rather than fully backtracking through the level.

The level ends outside with Beacon 9 visible and the module mounted to UNIT-07.

### What the player should understand

- The system failure extends across the city.
- A critical replacement module still exists.
- UNIT-07 now physically carries that module.
- Beacon 9 is the machine that needs it.

## Level 3 — Beacon 9

### Purpose

Level 3 is the delivery journey, the platforming climax, and the ending. UNIT-07 carries the module across the city, reaches Beacon 9, climbs into its maintenance infrastructure, and installs the module.

The module remains passive throughout the journey. Its first and only use is the final installation.

Target first-play time: roughly **8–11 minutes**.

### 1. Mission confirmation

Level 3 begins outside with the module mounted on UNIT-07 and Beacon 9 dominating the skyline.

A minimal system message can confirm the objective:

> BEACON 9  
> IGNITION ASSEMBLY OFFLINE  
> REPLACEMENT DETECTED

The game should not need a waypoint arrow. Beacon 9 itself is the waypoint.

### 2. Rooftop crossing

The first major section emphasizes horizontal momentum across rooftops, maintenance platforms, and building infrastructure. Platforms are longer and action sequences contain fewer pauses than earlier levels.

Beacon 9 should visibly grow as the player advances.

### 3. Transit viaduct

UNIT-07 crosses abandoned elevated transit or freight infrastructure: long decks, stopped vehicles or machinery, station structures, and rail-maintenance platforms.

This provides a different silhouette while keeping the same movement mechanics.

### 4. Utility canyon

The route drops lower between larger buildings and becomes temporarily enclosed again. A very short crouch passage may be used through collapsed service infrastructure, but long crouch tunnels should be avoided because crouching is intentionally slow.

The section ends with a close reveal of Beacon 9.

### 5. Beacon perimeter

UNIT-07 reaches the base infrastructure of Beacon 9. Difficulty briefly decreases so the structure can feel enormous relative to the player and the small module being carried.

Large maintenance decks and structural supports guide the player toward the ascent route.

### 6. Exterior ascent

This is the main platforming climax of the game. The ascent should be composed of several readable phrases rather than one continuous precision gauntlet:

- support truss traversal using mostly ordinary jumps,
- maintenance gantries combining ordinary and charged jumps,
- final ignition-access platforms containing the hardest but still fair charged-jump sequence.

Difficulty comes from longer combinations of mastered actions, not from pushing every jump to the movement system's theoretical limit.

### 7. Beacon interior

After the exterior ascent, the game becomes quiet again. UNIT-07 enters Beacon 9 through maintenance corridors and dormant machinery.

The enclosed geometry intentionally echoes the tutorial, but the narrative relationship is reversed: UNIT-07 is no longer being processed by a machine; it has arrived to repair one.

Platforming here should be easy.

### 8. Ignition chamber

The final chamber contains the failed ignition assembly and one obvious interface that matches the module UNIT-07 has carried since Level 2.

The approach contains little or no danger. The player should understand what needs to happen before a prompt appears.

### 9. Final installation — the module's only use

UNIT-07 removes the module and installs it into Beacon 9.

There should be a brief delay before anything happens. Activation then propagates outward in visible stages:

1. module/interface illumination,
2. nearby conduits,
3. ignition-chamber machinery,
4. Beacon 9's internal systems,
5. Beacon 9's exterior structure,
6. distant city infrastructure.

This is the first and only activation of the device in the game.

### 10. Ending

Do not immediately cut away after activation. Let the player see the result.

If practical, return control briefly while UNIT-07 stands in or near the restored Beacon 9 and the distant city begins lighting up.

Then fade to the ending/credits.

### What the player should understand

- The journey across Levels 2 and 3 had a concrete purpose.
- The small module was important because it restored a system vastly larger than UNIT-07.
- UNIT-07 has completed the task it chose or inherited after escaping the failed production process.

The game does not need to explain every detail of the world's history for this ending to feel complete.

## Gameplay and pacing rules

### Read → execute → breathe

Platforming should generally follow a repeating rhythm:

1. **Read**: the player can see the next short obstacle sequence.
2. **Execute**: perform two to four related movement actions.
3. **Breathe**: reach a wide, safe landing where the player can look at the environment and absorb story information.

The breathing spaces are where major narrative images belong: warehouse robots, the first Beacon 9 reveal, the reserve module, arrival at Beacon 9, and the ignition chamber.

### Difficulty progression

Later levels should become harder by combining familiar actions into longer sequences, not by making every gap dramatically wider.

- Ordinary jumps should remain the default.
- Charged jumps should be deliberate highlights.
- Mandatory crouch sections should be short.
- Major story reveals should usually happen in low-danger spaces.
- Mandatory set-pieces such as the warehouse collapse should not be hidden reaction-time death traps.

### Checkpoints

Because the numbered levels are substantially longer than the tutorial, use a few unobtrusive checkpoints per level. They do not need UI or save-game complexity; their purpose is simply to avoid forcing the player to replay several minutes after a mistake.

Suggested checkpoint locations:

- **Level 1**: warehouse landing; damaged warehouse edge.
- **Level 2**: reserve-facility entrance; module chamber; immediately after acquisition.
- **Level 3**: Beacon perimeter; midpoint of the exterior ascent; ignition-chamber entrance.

## Narrative rules

- Prefer environmental storytelling over exposition.
- Keep mandatory text terse and diegetic.
- Let architecture and broken automation create the platforming route naturally.
- Repeated robots, packaging, storage systems, labels, and machinery should reinforce UNIT-07's manufactured origin.
- Beacon 9 should be visible before it is fully explained.
- Reveal information in player order: **manufactured unit → one of many → wider failure → recover module → Beacon 9 target → installation**.
- Preserve uncertainty about the original cause of the failure unless a later version of the game has a concrete reason to explain it.
- Do not add combat, gadget puzzles, reusable module powers, or unrelated systems merely to lengthen the game.
- Make the game longer through distinct spaces, traversal rhythms, elevation changes, visual landmarks, and stronger environmental storytelling rather than through additional mechanics.

---

## Current scope

The intended school-project campaign is now:

**Calibration → Factory Escape → Recovery → Beacon 9**

Approximate first-play target:

- Tutorial: **1–2 minutes**
- Level 1: **5–7 minutes**
- Level 2: **7–9 minutes**
- Level 3: **8–11 minutes**
- Full game: approximately **21–29 minutes**

The priority is to finish and polish these four playable parts. Additional districts, bosses, combat systems, procedural campaigns, collectible systems, and larger lore branches are outside the current scope.
