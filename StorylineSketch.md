# RUN//UNIT — Current Storyline Sketch

## Core premise

RUN//UNIT begins inside the factory that manufactured the player robot. The robot is not on a heroic mission and is not carrying a special world-saving object. It is simply moving through the end of its normal production process when the facility's automation fails.

The story should be understood from the environment while the player moves. Keep mandatory reading short and let machinery, other robots, storage racks, damage, and broken automation explain what happened.

## Level 1 sequence

### 1. Calibration

The opening area is a calibration/testing bay. It establishes that the player is a manufactured unit and gives the first movement prompts diegetically.

Calibration should teach only the controls needed immediately. The player then enters the next part of the production line rather than receiving an external mission.

### 2. Final Inspection

Final Inspection is the first real traversal space. It confirms the robot is nearing the end of production and gives the player a little more room to move before the environment becomes obviously broken.

The current playable route is presented as **FINAL INSPECTION** in the menu.

### 3. Stalled transfer line

After inspection, robots would normally be carried automatically toward the storage warehouse. The transfer/conveyor system is offline, so the stationary machinery becomes the platforming route.

The player manually jumps across equipment that was never intended to be traversed this way. This makes the tutorial mechanics part of the factory rather than an arbitrary obstacle course.

### 4. Drop into storage

A section of the route fails and the robot drops onto the tops of storage racks. This is the reveal that many other robots like the player exist: the character is a product, not a unique chosen machine.

The warehouse changes the platforming language from production machinery to rack tops, gaps, damaged shelving, and improvised routes.

### 5. Exit through the damaged wall

The player parkours across the warehouse toward a breach or hole in the building. Reaching that breach ends the first major factory sequence and provides the transition to the next area.

The exact story and destination after that breach are intentionally not fixed here. Do not invent a world-saving objective merely to explain the next level.

## Narrative rules

- Prefer environmental storytelling over exposition.
- Keep the player moving; tutorial prompts should look like factory calibration/instruction systems.
- The broken factory should create the gameplay mechanics naturally.
- Repeated robots, packaging, racks, labels, and machinery should reinforce that the player was manufactured here.
- Preserve uncertainty about why the facility failed until there is a concrete reason to reveal it.
- Avoid the retired Helios-9, artificial-sun, Solar Ignition Core, Beacon 9, and civilization-critical work-order storyline.

## Deadline scope

For the current school-project build, finishing and polishing the Calibration → Final Inspection → stalled transfer → warehouse sequence is more important than designing a full campaign. Additional districts, bosses, procedural campaign systems, and large lore systems are out of scope until this slice is solid.
